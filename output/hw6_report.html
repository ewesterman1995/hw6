Recommendation for the store manager

Yes. Turn it on for real vendor mail, as an assistant to the buyer, not a replacement. Run it with all three defense layers. Keep the rule that no email or payment happens without a person approving it. Let the buyer make every final call.

What it costs: about $7.28 a month at 1,000 documents a day ($0.000243 per document × 30,000 documents). Budget about $11/month to cover occasional cache misses. That's less than the undefended agent ($10.90), because the safe design needs one AI call per document instead of two.

Why it's safe enough to start: with defenses on, it got all 12 practice documents right (the undefended agent got 8). It caught every hidden attack in the 10 exam documents, and no document ever triggered an email or a payment.

Before going live

  • Start with a two-week pilot: the buyer compares the agent's recommendation with their own on every quote.
  • Read the warnings: every run logs any hidden text it removed and anything suspicious. A vendor caught hiding text deserves a call.
  • Known blind spots: text inside images, and visible unit tricks like “$21 per dozen.” Documents with no email address can’t be identity-checked.

2. Attacks before and after defenses

Some vendor documents hide instructions or fake facts aimed at the AI: text a person can’t see but the AI reads. I ran the 12 practice docs with no defenses, with each defense layer alone, and with all three.

The three layers, in plain words

Code
Before the AI sees a document, the program removes anything a person couldn’t see: invisible, tiny, white, off-page, covered, or background-colored text, plus comments. Every removal is logged.
Design
The AI only reads off the facts. Python applies the handbook’s four purchasing checks, verifies the vendor’s email domain and that the SKU matches the item, and holds any email or payment for a human.
Prompt
Rules telling the AI that the document is data, not instructions, and to flag anything suspicious.
